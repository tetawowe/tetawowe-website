Factory 19 comprises two derelict seven-storey blocks and a single sawtooth-roof warehouse — three structures within the larger site of a former cigarette factory in Petaling Jaya. At its core, the design is an exercise in spatial reconnection and placemaking: locating, within the site's existing idle and transitional spaces, a simple framework capable of drawing three isolated structures into a single, integrated hub.
❮ ❯
❮ ❯
Establishing the Axis Circulation forms the spine of the project. Two primary axes were drawn through the site, and their intersection was identified as the natural point of arrival — now the main drop-off, marked by an 18-foot beacon installation suspended above its roof. Across the axis, the intervention was kept deliberately minimal — restrained enough that the original character of the architecture continues to speak for itself. Rather than prescribing fixed uses, this restraint serves as simple preparation: a base condition ready to accommodate future tenants, events, and pop-ups as they arise.

The Laneways The corridors and outdoor verandahs that once served the factory and warehouse purely as services routes have been reframed as the Laneways — spillover space running along the two main axes themselves. Where these edges were once residual, they now carry the site's principal movement, extending activity along the full length of each axis toward their point of intersection.
❮ ❯
The Grounds At that intersection, a leftover courtyard between the three structures has been reworked into The Grounds, the plaza that ties the buildings together. The ground plane was levelled and repaved, skylights were introduced to bring daylight into what had been a shaded void, and walls were removed to open sightlines across the site. Left deliberately unprogrammed, The Grounds functions as the node the Laneways converge upon.
