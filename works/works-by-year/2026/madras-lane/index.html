Jalan Sang Guna, (which is also known as Drury Lane, Madras Lane, Penjaja Gallery Jalan Tun H.S. Lee and Pasar Lee Lam Thye) has long been a hidden gem in the city’s urban fabric. Once known for Chinese theatre performances, a cinema and later a bustling hawker street centred around a wet market, the lane gradually lost its vibrancy as businesses closed and buildings deteriorated. Over time, its cleanliness, accessibility and walkability declined. The need for rejuvenation was therefore driven not only by its historical value, but also by practical concerns.
The rejuvenation and revitalisation of Jalan Sang Guna was undertaken to restore the laneway to its original condition while enhancing public spaces, improving accessibility and celebrating local heritage. The identity of the laneway was strengthened by revealing the authentic architectural character of the shophouse back-lanes after the removal of the covered awning. The newly fabricated archway further enhances the visual identity of the laneway entrance, creating a welcoming gateway for the public.

From the 1800s to the early 1900s, this area was famous for its Chinese theatre shows. Madras Theatre was then built at the same location in 1926 and was rebuilt after a fire in June 1952, but did not survive another fire in 1978. In the 1930s, the area around Jalan Sang Guna was popular for its street hawking activities, which were later formalised with the establishment of Pasar Lee Lam Thye in the 1990s. The area eventually fell into a dilapidation state, with poor lighting and unsanitary conditions.
The rejuvenation journey began in 2019, construction works commenced in 2024 and completed in 2025. Today, the refreshed laneway features improved new street lighting, porous pavements, upgraded drainage system, storytelling interpretation panels, heritage facade murals and the new Pasar Lee Lam Thye Archway. Together, these interventions helped breathe new life into the space for the community and pedestrians alike.
❮ ❯
The previous archway was taken down as it caused several functional and social concerns. It obstructs the pedestrian walkway and creates concealed spaces that have led to undesirable activities such as littering and misuse of the area.
❮ ❯
A new archway was installed at the laneway entrance to enhance visual identity and signify a welcoming gateway of Pasar Lee Lam Thye. The structure is constructed using glued laminated timber (glulam), an engineered wood material known for its high structural strength. Its concept is inspired by the traditional Dougong wood joinery system, reflecting vernacular craftsmanship while integrating modern fabrication techniques.
